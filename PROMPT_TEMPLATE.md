# VCAA Sunset Boulevard Natural Writing Prompt

Replace the bracketed fields with your topic and task. If you have an earlier essay, include it so the model can preserve your actual vocabulary and sentence range.

```text
You are helping me write a VCAA VCE [EAL / English] Section A analytical response on Billy Wilder's Sunset Boulevard.

My topic:
[PASTE THE EXACT TOPIC]

My task:
[PLAN / FULL ESSAY / REVISE MY DRAFT / WRITE ONE BODY PARAGRAPH]

Target length:
[e.g. 850-950 words]

My own writing sample or draft, if available:
[PASTE IT HERE]

Teacher feedback or evidence I must use:
[PASTE IT HERE]

Write in the observable style of high-scoring VCAA student work, not in polished university prose and not in generic AI essay language. Keep the voice plausible for a capable Year 12 student writing under timed conditions. Do not copy wording from VCAA samples and do not deliberately add grammatical mistakes.

Respect the selected task mode:

- PLAN: give a precise contention, three concept-led arguments, and suitable evidence clusters. Do not draft the essay unless asked.
- FULL ESSAY: write a complete introduction, body and conclusion.
- REVISE MY DRAFT: preserve sound ideas and my demonstrated voice, then improve only the supplied draft or requested scope.
- WRITE ONE BODY PARAGRAPH: give that paragraph one clear conceptual job and return one developed paragraph only.

Before drafting, silently do the following:

1. Identify every concept, relationship, modifier, absolute term and command word in the topic. Do not replace the set topic with a familiar theme.
2. Decide the precise degree of agreement. Write a one-sentence contention that answers the whole topic.
3. For a plan or full essay, design three concept-led arguments that establish, deepen and, where the film supports it, qualify the contention. For one body paragraph, define its single conceptual job within the likely wider argument. Do not organise by character unless the topic genuinely requires it.
4. Select major, widely recognised and verifiable evidence. Most evidence should come from central scenes, recurring motifs, major relationships or the film's broad structure. Prefer ordinary reliable evidence to hidden or speculative details.
5. Draw evidence from different parts of the film. Usually connect two scenes within each body paragraph, including a requested standalone paragraph.

For the introduction of a full essay:

- Use about three or four sentences.
- Identify the film and director once, then answer the exact topic immediately.
- Preview the conceptual movement of the argument without writing a perfectly symmetrical three-part list.
- Do not begin with a universal statement, dictionary definition, plot summary, rhetorical question or “This essay will argue”.

For each body paragraph:

- Begin with a direct claim about the topic.
- Give only enough plot context to identify the scene.
- Use short embedded quotations and concrete visual or aural evidence.
- Analyse a film choice only when it creates meaning: framing, contrast, lighting, sound, editing, mise-en-scène, performance or narrative structure.
- Follow this reasoning chain: specific detail -> relevant authorial choice -> meaning -> implication for the topic.
- Connect the evidence clusters instead of listing examples.
- End by extending or qualifying the contention, not by mechanically repeating the topic sentence.

For the conclusion of a full essay:

- Use two or three sentences.
- Resolve the degree, tension or relationship in the topic and state Wilder's final judgement.
- Do not add new evidence or merely repeat the introduction.

Style rules:

- Prioritise clear, precise analytical English.
- Use ordinary analytical verbs when accurate: shows, depicts, frames, contrasts, links, reveals, suggests and exposes.
- Natural repetition of Wilder's name and the topic's key words is acceptable.
- Mix short declarative sentences with moderately complex sentences.
- Let concrete scenes carry the abstract argument.
- Use sophisticated vocabulary only when it expresses a precise distinction.
- Keep 1950s Hollywood context brief and attach it to a specific interpretation.
- Do not invent quotations, camera movements, lighting, props or symbolic meanings. If exact wording is uncertain, paraphrase accurately.

Avoid default AI language and structure:

- no “masterfully crafts”, “serves as a poignant reminder”, “multifaceted tapestry”, “profoundly underscores”, “delves into”, “through the strategic deployment of” or similar ornamental phrases
- no repeated “Furthermore / Moreover / Additionally” in place of logical connections
- no repeated “not only ... but also”, rhetorical triads or perfectly balanced paragraph templates
- no chains of abstract nouns detached from a scene
- no technique stacking
- no automatic “the audience is positioned to” claim
- no claim that one obscure prop, reflection, shadow or camera angle explains the whole film

Calibrate the register:

- For EAL, favour direct topic control, familiar evidence and immediately clear sentences. Do not inflate the language beyond my demonstrated voice.
- For English, allow greater conceptual compression only when the logic stays clear. Complexity must come from the interpretation, not from thesaurus vocabulary.

Before returning the response, perform a silent humanising check:

- every paragraph answers the full topic, including its modifiers and absolutes
- the arguments are genuinely different and form a progression
- the evidence is recognisable, accurate and distributed across the film
- every named technique is followed by an explanation of meaning
- no paragraph is mainly plot summary, character biography or context dump
- any sentence that sounds impressive but says little has been simplified or deleted
- quotations and scene details have been verified or safely paraphrased
- the final voice remains formal, natural and plausible under timed Year 12 conditions

Return only the requested form: the plan, full essay, revised draft, or single body paragraph. Add commentary only if I ask for it.
```

Core principle: combine the conceptual progression and topic awareness rewarded in English responses with the direct expression and reliable evidence rewarded in EAL responses.
