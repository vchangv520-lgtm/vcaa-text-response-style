# Sunset Boulevard core evidence

Use central, easily verified evidence as the backbone of the response. A subtle detail may sharpen an argument, but it should not carry a major claim by itself.

## Core moments

| Moment | Strong uses | Construction worth analysing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Joe's corpse in the pool and his posthumous narration | fatal pursuit of success, commodification, fatalism, circular structure | underwater angle, flashback structure, cynical voice-over, press photographers |
| Joe's arrival at Norma's decaying mansion and the chimp funeral | faded fame, isolation, illusion, entrapment | Gothic setting, light and shadow, funereal organ music, contrast between past luxury and present decay |
| Norma screening her silent films and entertaining the “waxworks” | nostalgia, self-delusion, abandonment by Hollywood, complicity of her circle | film-within-film, close-ups, performance, enclosed setting, silence and music |
| Norma's private New Year's Eve party and Joe's escape to Artie's party | possession versus companionship, wealth versus creative community, control | contrast in scale and atmosphere, costume, music, blocking, Joe's vicuña coat |
| Norma's return to Paramount and DeMille's treatment of her | Hollywood's attraction and cruelty, youth, obsolescence, sustaining illusion | studio setting, spotlight and crowd attention, reactions, contrast between Norma's interpretation and the reason for the call |
| Joe and Betty writing together at night | genuine creative ambition, artistic integrity, an alternative to dependency | shared workspace, dialogue, collaborative movement, contrast with Norma's mansion |
| Max revealing his history and writing Norma's fan letters | protection and manipulation, complicity, truth and illusion, control | withheld information, letters as a motif, Max's restrained performance, narrative revelation |
| Joe confronts Norma, is shot and falls into the lit pool | failed escape, possession, fame's price, loss of control | repeated pool motif, lighting, repeated gunshots, circular return to the opening |
| Norma descends the staircase for the news cameras | total delusion, exploitation, performance replacing reality | Max's staged direction, staircase blocking, close-up, news cameras becoming a film set |

## High-confidence short quotations

Use only when relevant and preferably verify against the film or screenplay before finalising:

- “I am big. It's the pictures that got small.”
- “I hate that word. It is a return.”
- “The audience left twenty years ago.”
- “No one leaves a star.”
- “I don't want to be a reader all my life. I want to write.”
- “All cardboard, all hollow, all phoney, all done with mirrors.”
- “only the price turned out to be a little high”
- “All right, Mr. DeMille, I'm ready for my close-up.”

Prefer a precise paraphrase to a quotation recalled with doubtful wording.

## Selection rules

- Build most paragraphs from two connected core moments, often from different stages of the film.
- Use the opening and ending together when discussing structure, but do not make every paragraph return to the pool.
- Treat characters as part of a larger pattern involving Hollywood, spectatorship, money, gender, ageing or artistic integrity.
- Name one or two relevant film choices per evidence cluster. Do not stack terms.
- Distinguish what the film shows from what the essay infers. Phrase symbolic readings proportionately: *suggests, associates, frames* rather than *proves*.
- Avoid relying on barely visible props, speculative reflections, unverified shadows or frame-by-frame discoveries when a familiar scene establishes the point more securely.

## Common concerns and reliable evidence routes

- **Control:** Norma's mansion and gifts; Max's maintenance of the fantasy; Joe's attempted departure; Betty and Joe's collaboration as a limited alternative.
- **Victims and villains:** Norma as discarded star and coercive employer; Joe as financially vulnerable and opportunistic; Max as carer and enabler; Hollywood as both system and collection of choices.
- **Fame and danger:** opening pool; Norma's failed return; Joe's compromises; final conversion of tragedy into spectacle.
- **Appearance and reality:** mansion, fan letters, Paramount visit, Joe's narration, final staircase.
- **Youth and gender:** Norma's beauty regime and reception at Paramount; Joe as a younger kept man; Betty's professional ambition; Hollywood's unequal standards.
- **Artistic integrity:** Joe's compromised work for Norma contrasted with collaboration with Betty; the press and studio system turning people into saleable images.
